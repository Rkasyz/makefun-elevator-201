A one-line entry for the make.fun competition "What the Elevator
Would Say": the funniest thing an elevator would say if it could talk,
spoken in the elevator's own voice.

## The entry (110 characters)

> Going up? I would love to, but someone pressed every button, so we are visiting all fourteen floors of regret.

110 characters, one line, inside the gate of 1–120 characters. The
joke opens with the elevator's own question ("Going up?"), then turns
on its own job: every button pressed means a stop at every floor, and
the ride becomes a tour of "all fourteen floors of regret". The voice
could come from no other machine — buttons, floors, going up — and the
twist lands on the last two words. Against the judge's brief: the line
is the elevator speaking, not a narrator describing one; the joke lands
on the first read with no setup to explain; every word is doing
elevator work; and the "floors of regret" turn is fresh rather than a
recycled elevator pun.

## How to run

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out python3 main.py
```

This writes `entry.txt` into the output directory, containing exactly
the line above, with no trailing newline.
